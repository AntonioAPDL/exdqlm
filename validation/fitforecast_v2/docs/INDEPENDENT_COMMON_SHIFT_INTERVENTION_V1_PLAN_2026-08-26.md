# Independent Q-DESN common-shift intervention v1

## Purpose

This phase follows the completed 21-chain origin-horizon attribution campaign. It
does not reopen the DESN or `tau0` search. Its purpose is to determine whether the
two Normal lower-tail cases identified as location-error dominant can be improved
by a targeted location/design intervention, and whether their wide posterior
metric intervals are primarily generated by a draw-level common shift.

## Frozen evidence

The source authority is
`independent_origin_horizon_attribution_v1_full_20260826_021943`. That campaign
completed 21/21 chains and found:

- 93.1--96.1% of aggregate forecast-MAE variance was cross-group covariance;
- late/early MAE ratios were 0.970--1.087;
- RHS-scale absolute median associations with MAE were 0.018--0.077;
- no cell passed the predeclared `tau0` causal gate;
- `imi_v1_source_073` and `imi_v1_source_075` were location-error dominant.

The current experiment therefore targets only those two Normal, `p=0.05` cells.
Their authoritative DESN specifications, priors, likelihood implementations,
reservoir seeds, chain seeds, windows, forecast origins, and MCMC budgets remain
unchanged.

## Estimands

For posterior quantile draw `q^(s)` and oracle path `q*`, define the posterior
mean path `qbar` and two diagnostic counterfactuals:

1. Common-shift removed: subtract the mean target-wise deviation of each draw
   from `qbar`. This preserves the posterior mean location and measures how much
   uncertainty is caused by coherent draw-level motion across forecast targets.
2. Oracle-location corrected: subtract the mean error of each draw relative to
   `q*`. This is deliberately non-deployable. It measures the maximum opportunity
   for a location correction and must never be reported as model performance.

For observed and counterfactual paths, compute draw-specific forecast MAE and
forecast check loss, equal-tailed 95% intervals, mean ratios, variance ratios,
and interval-width ratios. The full forecast-draw matrix exists only in memory;
compact CSV summaries are retained.

## Gates

- Common-mode gate: pooled MAE variance ratio <= 0.75 and every chain <= 0.85.
- Location-bias gate: pooled oracle-corrected MAE mean ratio <= 0.90.
- Three independently seeded chains are required per cell.
- A gate authorizes design of the next matched intervention; it does not authorize
  article promotion, a `tau0` change, or an automatic follow-up launch.
- Forecast MAE and check loss are co-primary diagnostic outcomes. Narrowing an
  interval without improving its risk is not success.

## Execution

The campaign contains six CPU-pinned, single-threaded MCMC jobs. It reuses the
current interval worker and source trajectories, adds the intervention artifacts
during in-memory metric computation, and removes fitted-model binaries after the
required summaries are written. Materialization records config hashes, source
authority, CPU assignment, and storage policy. The closeout pools chains by cell
and emits a case-specific decision.

## Interpretation and next stage

- If the location-bias gate passes, construct a matched location/design campaign
  for that cell only, beginning with intercept treatment and training-only
  location calibration while holding the reservoir and `tau0` fixed.
- If only the common-mode gate passes, identify the parameterization producing
  coherent shifts before any refit.
- If neither gate passes, retain the authoritative model and do not spend further
  compute on this mechanism.
- A future `tau0` experiment requires new direct evidence linking RHS scales to
  forecast risk; this phase cannot authorize it.

No article metric changes are permitted in this phase. Any later performance
promotion must use an actual deployable model fit and a strict case-specific
forecast improvement under the fixed evaluation protocol.
